So, what is ful?

Ful medames (pronounced "fool med-ah-mess") is Egypt's national dish: slow-cooked fava beans, mashed with olive oil, lemon, garlic, and cumin. It's been breakfast on Egyptian streets for thousands of years, served from carts and corner shops right alongside falafel.

Traditionally it takes an overnight soak and a long simmer. We make the same dish, the same way, we just do the soaking and simmering before it ever reaches your fridge.

Ancient.
One of the oldest recorded dishes in the world, eaten in Egypt since the time of the Pharaohs.
Filling.
Built on fava beans: more protein and fiber per cup than the chickpeas in hummus.
Unfussy.
Usually eaten with bread, olive oil, and whatever's on the table. We kept that spirit, just in a tub.

Our story

A USC student, his grandmother's recipe, and a tub.

Asher Dubin grew up in Cairo eating ful medames most mornings of his life, made the way his grandmother made it: slow-cooked fava beans, good olive oil, and whatever flavor she felt like that day.

When he came to Los Angeles to study business at USC, he couldn't find it anywhere, not at a grocery store, not at a restaurant. So he started making small batches in his apartment kitchen for friends, then for their friends. Shalabean is that recipe, made shelf-stable for a refrigerator aisle instead of a stovetop.

"I want people here to get to try the dish I grew up on, the same way I grew up eating it. Not a fusion version, not a smoothie bowl version. Just ful, the way my family makes it."

— Asher Dubin, Founder, USC '26
